The three Chinese monks Must know

Why they came: to collect Buddhist texts, visit places linked to the Buddha, and learn from Indian teachers.

MonkWhen / routeRemember
Faxian399–413 · overland there, by sea backCame for Vinaya texts (monastery rules). Described Gupta India. Returned via Sri Lanka on a merchant ship, a scary voyage. Showed that monks and merchants helped each other.
Xuanzang629–645 · overland"The greatest traveller". Took back 657 texts. Met King Harsha at Kanauj; studied at Nalanda. Gave India the Chinese name "Yindu". Was also a diplomat for the Tang emperor. His notes help archaeologists today.
Yijing671–695 · by seaStudied at Srivijaya (Sumatra) on the way. Recorded daily monastery rules. Wrote short lives of 56 Chinese monks who went to India.

"Borderland complex"

Chinese monks felt China was on the edge of the Buddhist world, far from the Buddha's land. Some even wished not to be reborn in China.

Faxian's companion stayed in India for good.

Their big impact

They made China see India as a civilised, holy land (high praise, since the Chinese usually called foreigners "barbarians"). They also show monks travelling with merchants, an early sign of trade and religion moving together (Sessions 7–9).

The Arab scholar: al-Shahrastani (12th c.) Must know

Book: Kitab al-Milal wa'l-Nihal ("Book of Religions and Sects"), a chapter on Indian religions. M.S. Khan evaluates it.

Strengths

  • Systematic: neatly classified Indian religions
  • Fair tone: did not insult Indian religions
  • Unique details on Buddhism
  • Understood that Hindus believe God himself comes down (avatar) instead of sending a prophet with a book
but

Weaknesses

  • Never visited India; knew no Sanskrit
  • Copied older Arabic books (secondary sources)
  • Errors: thought "Brahma" was a man's name; treated Buddhism as a branch of Hinduism
  • Left out the Vedas and Jainism; judged India through Islamic ideas

Write it like this (10 marks)
Q. How useful are foreign travellers' accounts as sources for Indian history?

Where Indian records are scarce, foreign accounts are very useful, but travel writing mixes observation, memory, interpretation and opinion, so it is both evidence and interpretation.

The Chinese monks were eyewitnesses. Faxian described Gupta India and his sea voyage home; Xuanzang described Harsha's Kanauj and Nalanda and brought back 657 texts; Yijing recorded monastery life and studied at Srivijaya. They also show monks travelling with merchants. But they saw India through a Buddhist lens.

Al-Shahrastani systematically classified Indian religions, but he never visited India, knew no Sanskrit, relied on older Arabic books and made errors (e.g. "Brahma" as a man). Al-Biruni, who learned Sanskrit and used original texts, is more reliable.

So historians must corroborate these accounts with inscriptions, coins and archaeology. Used critically, they also show that India was connected to Asia, not isolated.
