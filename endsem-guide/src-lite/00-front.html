History Endsem: Exam Essentials

Every key point from the endsem readings and PPTs (Sessions 12–15 and 17–22), explained in simple words with an example for each idea, a map or diagram per session, and a ready model answer at the end of each session. Small side details are marked "Skip".

How to use this guide (read in 1 minute)

Each session has the same 5 parts

  1. In one line: the whole session in one sentence.
  2. Picture it: a map or diagram.
  3. All key points: each keyword in simple words, with an e.g.
  4. Names & dates: only the ones worth writing in an answer.
  5. Write it like this: a ready model answer you can adapt.

The exam

  • Final exam = 35% of your grade.
  • Midsem pattern (Lecture 16): A 10 MCQs × ½ = 5 · B 3 of 5 short answers × 10 = 30 · C 1 essay × 15 = 15. Closed book, 2 hours.
  • Expect the same unless told otherwise: check with the professor.

The professor wants: paragraphs (not bullets), the scholar's name, examples (people, places, dates), and one "but…".

The 4-step formula for every answer 1. Say what the scholar argues in one line ("Nadri argues that…"). 2. Explain 2–3 points, each with an example. 3. Add one "but" (a limit or criticism). 4. End with one line linking to the big course idea (e.g. "empire was a process, not a single event").

The endsem on one page

Six phrases that fit almost any answer

  • "Connection does not mean sameness" (or equality).
  • "The forms of connection changed; connection continued."
  • "Read empire as a process, not a single event."
  • "Commerce and belief travelled together."
  • "Technology is inseparable from power."
  • "Sources are never neutral."
