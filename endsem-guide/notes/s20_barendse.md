# S20 René J. Barendse, "Port Cities in the Gulf and the Red Sea During the Long Eighteenth Century (c.1720–c.1840): General Characteristics and Some Comparisons with Southeast Asia" (Schottenhammer ed. vol I ch 14, pp343–374) [RB5]. Summary of his The Arabian Seas 1700–1763 (4 vols, Brill 2009).
- Thesis: long 18th c in the Arabian seas = Anthony (Tony) Reid's "LAST STAND OF ASIAN AUTONOMIES": despite European colonialism, "traditional" Asian trade remained equal or EXPANDED; linked to global "take-off" into modern growth.
- Great transformation only mid-19th c: 1860s cotton boom (American Civil War) — capital from same Bania merchants who traded to Persia, Red Sea, Africa → Bombay mills; steam navigation (P&O) — Arab/Indian shipowners couldn't compete; Raj contracts to British firms; SUEZ → Red Sea dominates; Gulf became "sleepy, placid backwater", "mare britanica". Red Sea vs Gulf rivalry (one closes, other opens). 1830s Red Sea shipping & shipbuilding boom at Jedda & Hadramawt (not British).
- Irfan Habib's warning: don't study colonial economy "without perceiving colonialism"; but colonial officials (Gulf residents) overrated own influence. 18th c now seen as age of prosperity in Middle East & India (not "dark age" — Habib still sees it so); Iran exception (1722 Safavid collapse, Nadir Shah's wars; Qajars weaker). Bengal shipping boomed after 1757 but living standards fell (like Java). Arabian seas less tightly controlled than Straits of Malacca (Dutch/British); porous frontiers (Tagliacozzo). Omani imams & Qajars played off Europeans (Great Game; "French bogey"); Oman staunch British ally since 1650. Indian traders under Union Jack = hostages; consuls' daily work interceding for Indian merchants. Critical events NOT European: 1722 Safavid collapse, Omani civil war 1720s–30s, Qajar rule over Gulf, Wahhabi inroads into Muscat 1820s (imams moved seat to Zanzibar), Mehmed Ali's Egypt into Hijaz & Yemen. → can't divide into pre-colonial/colonial; long 18th c = 1722 to 1840s: no centralised regime over Gulf or Red Sea, trade in same Asian hands.
- PORT CITIES small: Basra 40–60,000 (largest Gulf); Bandar Abbas 20,000 at 17th c height → few thousand; Kung/Lingeh ~5000; Muscat 8000 (Portuguese) → 30,000 late 18th → ~50,000 19th c; Kuwait 5–6000; al-Mukka ≤10,000; Jedda ~40,000; Aden 3000 at British occupation (1839) — "bunkering station in the middle of nowhere". Dwarfed by interior: Baghdad 120,000; Isfahan 200,000 (Safavid); Tehran 40,000 → 350,000. Reasons: food supply (Muscat rice from Mangalore/Kanara, later Bengal — British-controlled → Busaidis must stay friendly; Bengal starving yet food exporter), epidemics (disease interstices; African slaves preferred as "more resistant"); Bushire "most detestable city" (1840s, 110–125°F).
- Some ports = shifting fairs (Hudayda — Bait al-Faki coffee fairs; Bandar Lingeh — Lar, Kerman, Shiraz) like European fairs (Bergen op Zoom, Leipzig; Leipzig–Lingeh fair network, Greek & Serbian merchants) — older "cultural and ecological type" view. But most had strong corporate identity & municipal pride: Suhar (Roman times), Sur, Muscat, Aden (2000 yrs), Bahrain (Babylonian), Rig; local surroundings (dates, horses, grain — Rig/Bushire exported grain 16th–19th c; Aden frankincense). Hormuz "blown away as if by volcanic eruption" (Steensgaard, 1620s) → people moved to Bandar Abbas. Kuwait 1750s: new caravan route to Aleppo + sulphur mine (ballast) → oceanic centre.
- Functions: distribution (hinterland & "foreland"), banking/clearance, "primary spot"/STAPLE markets (centralised information + price fixing → lower transaction costs, like Antwerp/Amsterdam); Bandar Abbas → by 1780s Kuwait (lower Gulf, Syria transit) & Muscat; Gulf prices level (4–5% difference); Jedda for Red Sea. Hinterland regimes (Safavid Bandar Abbas, Mamluk Basra) charged higher customs (farmed, squeezed) but protected roads (caravanserai revenues); Gujarati banias in Yemen punished; Khoja & Bohra Muslims less so; tax-farmer valis were merchants; merchant strikes & bribing sheikhs. Nominal central control; Bushire under Mazkur sheikhs; Bandar Abbas sheikh wavered between shah & imam of Oman. Like Malacca Straits petty sultanates. LEGAL PLURALISM best safeguarded by city-states; ruler as merchant (17th c Aceh; al-Sabah of Kuwait leading merchants; sheikhs warlords/pirates + merchants). British agents presided over merchant courts for British subjects/protégés (mostly Indians); Safavids complained British sheltered Indian merchants under Union Jack.
- Gulf "thoroughly commercialised... very capitalist" society; desert warlords → shipowners; coast vs interior (Omani honour vs open coast). Migratory, cosmopolitan population (Zanzibar description; Aden 1830 half Jews, Somalis; Mukka Somali/Ethiopian merchants & slaves, slave concubines). Seafaring tribes al-Huwaila & Bani Main (Iranian shore; fought Portuguese & British; carrying trade); Hadramawt marine elites linked to SE Asia & E Africa; Gulf Arab chiefs egalitarian. Seasonal labour migration (Mukka highlanders rotate). INDIAN COMMUNITY: prostitutes to tycoons, priests; temples allowed in Iran, private worship only in Yemen; richest group in Mukka; Gujarati Banias, Bohras, Sayyids — but most Gulf banias from SIND; Khatris & Marwaris (Delhi, Bikaner, Shekhawati).
- Causes of Asian prosperity: expansion of tilled land (Egypt, Iraq), Iran's recovery → rural demand; drop in transport & handling costs; extended sailing season (May–July vs April in 16th c); lower transaction costs — interest rates & bill tariffs fell, maritime INSURANCE common by mid-18th c (bottomry rates custom-based); interlocking banking Malacca–Basra–Comoros; 1780s Pune firms drew bills on Istanbul (16th c needed stages Pune→Cambay→Basra→Aleppo); letter of credence → credit in Suez, Basra, Tellicherry, Madagascar; Islamic bill of exchange vs Indian HUNDI — by 1730s EIC ran Mukka trade entirely in bills endorsable at Surat. External: (1) CHINA's economic expansion (trepang from Makassar, Gujarat cotton, Malabar pepper, opium — revived Daman; China ~20% of Bombay customs by early 19th c); (2) AMERICAS — slave demand (Cuba, Brazil, USA): East Africa slave exports 3000/yr (early 18th c) → 20–30,000 (early 19th c) — emptied hinterland; Maria Theresa thaler main coin. Main driver: middle-class demand in Middle East, Swahili coast, South Asia — Red Sea trade quadrupled 17th→18th c; coffee to Europe declined after 1730 but Indian textiles & tobacco (Hijaz) boomed; Swahili coast boom after 1730 (cowries for India) → Omani clove/nutmeg plantations → Zanzibar nutmeg island by 1820s, slave destination.
- Capital in same hands: Chinese kongsi & rise of Singapore ("as much a Chinese commercial enclave as British"); Zanzibar trade in closed coterie of Bania merchants from Kutch Mandvi, Bhavnagar, Porbandar (since 16th c); Portuguese E Africa run by Kathiawar Banias & Luso-Indian Goan firms (trading with Brazil) — "portfolio capitalists".
- COUNTRY TRADE & empire: "Anglo/Bania alliance" & "Anglo/Indian empire" until 1880s; by 1780 bulk of IO shipping British-OPERATED but financed by Indian/Armenian/Arab consortia (respondentia bills, supercargo "factorage" 10% share, Indian underwriters, Arab/Iranian freight & attorneys). Legal interface: English common law, Hindu contract law, sharia — British appealed to Mughal courts; problem in Red Sea sharia courts (Jedda main British port by 1740, no farmans). Late 18th c British trade self-financing via AGENCY HOUSES (Company officials' savings; remittance by bills = "drain"); Forbes & Co ~30 ships from Bombay. Against Edward Ingram (British interest purely political): country trade alive; diplomacy protected country traders; political agents invested & earned consulage. Why British country trade rose? Lower customs, Royal Navy protection → cheaper credit, lower profit rates. Bulk of Gulf/Red Sea trade LOCAL (1 long-distance ship to 20–30 short-haul); dates, sheep, wood, tobacco; dhow trade prospered into 20th c; Basra merchants used Ottomans to exclude British (pre-WWI).
- Change & continuity: 19th c "first globalisation" — Bombay tycoons Jamsetji Jejeebhoy, Mhamai family of Goa; integrated Ottoman/Iranian markets; Bombay, Alexandria, Marseille, London; westernised bourgeoisie (Copts, Armenians, Greeks first) in Isfahan, Tehran, Baghdad, Jedda; British campaign vs Qawasim ("trucial coast") protected Bombay merchants' ships (Bania & Parsi-owned); British had little to teach in accounting/finance — "just as capitalistic"; Islamic law format constraints; British still depended on Indian, Armenian, Iranian brokers; Armenians since 16th c branches abroad. Aden resident Stafford Haines (1830s) ran town with coterie (Mulla Jafar, Parsi Surabji Cowraji...). Kerala pepper to Middle East shipped by Muscatis at Kozhikode & Mappilas (intermarried). Indian textiles 40% of Bushire imports (1840s), ~half of Aden's (1850s); Manchester modest until 1830s–40s. Beads for ivory → "beads inflation" 1840s.
- Conclusion: beware both long continuities and sharp breaks; K.N. Chaudhuri's Asia before Europe ends in 1757 — wrong; agrees with SUGATA BOSE: IO became MORE of a unity in colonial period — mass Indian emigration (Mauritius "little India", E Africa, SE Asia, S Africa), Indian-manned garrisons & navy; Gulf & Red Sea = India's periphery (agencies run by Government of India till 1880s) → IO more "Indian" than ever. In Gulf the "high tide of Empire" after 1870s was OTTOMAN (telegraph, machine gun, steamship); British supported status quo (Kuwait's independence, not "13th province of Iraq").
