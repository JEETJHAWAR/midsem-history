# S18 Holden Furber, Rival Empires of Trade in the Orient, 1600–1800 (1976) ch 3 "The Triumph of Tea: The Making of British Power" pp125–184 [R14]
- 1713 Peace of Utrecht → 3 decades of calm in eastern seas; Walpole kept Britain out of war; alliance of "maritime powers" (Britain, Austria, Netherlands); Anglo-Dutch wars unthinkable; Dutch names on English Company stock lists. 1740s Anglo-French "duel for empire" (Dupleix) → British power by century's end. Throughout: ever-increasing CHINA trade (tea, porcelain, chinoiserie) in background.
- Dutch indirect China contact via Batavia (Formosa 1624–61; sole Europeans in Japan after 1641); Kangxi opened ports 1685 just as European tea demand grew; VOC clung to transshipment via Batavia (spoiled tea flavour); direct trade only 1729, gave up 1734 → English "lion's share". First tea in England 1664 (2 lb 2 oz to Charles II); 1667 first order 100 lb; Amoy voyages (Fukien → word "tea" vs Cantonese "cha"); 1715 Madras country ship Anne seized Siamese junk at Amoy → end of Amoy; 1716 regular council of SUPERCARGOES at Canton → system until 1833. Tea imports >100,000 lb by 1706; within 75 years Canton "China" goods ≈ value of all "India" goods.
- United EIC (merger 1709) stable: Bank of England support, Parliament support, India House Leadenhall St, Blackwall docks; 24 directors; silver from Amsterdam & Hamburg, iron/copper from Baltic, woollens, lead; hired ships (East Indiamen life 4–6 seasons); "private and privilege" trade of officers (outward marine stores, gadgets; homeward fine silks/cottons) — voyage preference: "Coast and Bay" (Madras/Bengal) > China > Bombay. 1717–18 turning point: ships 100→150, tonnage 41,000→62,000 (1717–27); sales exceeded costs by ~£1m/yr by 1727; £30m surplus 1713–43; India stock second only to Bank stock (186 in 1743); dividends 10%→7%. Calico acts against home consumption but re-export markets (Poland, Norway, Russia, Balkans, Levant); "ungovernable female passion" for calicoes; coarse cottons for slave trade (Asiento). Calico imports 500,000 pieces/yr (1701–10) → 980,000 (1731–40); tea sales £116,000 (1712) → £348,000 (1744). Shift of British activity west coast → EAST coast; Calcutta separate presidency (Fort William) — largest emporium on Hugli; 1717 John Surman embassy → Farrukhsiyar's farman (duty-free trade for 3,000 Rs; dastaks) + zamindari rights over Calcutta villages (later base of power). Able viceroys Murshid Quli Khan (1717–27), Shuja-ud-din (1727–39); Jagat Seth banking family controlled currency, blocked a Calcutta mint. Country captains in Calcutta punch houses 15–20 (1713) → 40–50 (1743); British country ships outnumber French 2:1; carry Malayan tin & Japan copper (once Dutch-controlled). Bengal raw silk 175,000 lb & saltpetre exports. Silver from London to Bengal £75,000 (c.1713) → £100,000 (c.1743). Madras: Fort St George, governor ruled weavers' countryside, "white" & "black" towns; worried about French at Pondicherry & Maratha incursions.
- PLASSEY: Siraj-ud-daula; news of Anglo-French war 1757; Admiral Watson took Chandernagore (March 1757); Ahmad Shah Abdali threat; Select Committee decided to depose nawab 23 Apr 1757; Seths & non-Muslim elites (Hindu, Parsi, Sikh) saw British as means to power; conspirators chose MIR JAFAR; Amin Chand (Omichund) demanded 5% → fake treaty with forged Watson signature (Clive's connivance). Plassey 23 June 1757: Clive's 2000 sepoys, 900 Europeans, 100 topasses, 8 guns vs nawab's 50,000 (Mir Murdan 12,000 sepoys + 50 French); rain silenced nawab's guns; Major Kilpatrick's initiative; Mir Jafar's troops held back; nawab fled on camel. "Less fighting... than at any other 'decisive battle'". Rewards: Clive £234,000, Watts £117,000, councillors £12,000 each, subaltern £3000. "Europeans could not intervene in Indian politics and remain mere traders" → one must be the "COUNTRY POWER"; 1759 Dutch expedition (700 Europeans + 800 Buginese) crushed by Francis Forde ("Dear Forde, fight 'em immediately"). Lally lost; Wandiwash 22/23 Jan 1760 (Coote captured Bussy); Pondicherry surrendered Jan 1761; Manila captured 1762; Treaty of Paris 1763 (French factories restored, no troops in Bengal).
- 1761 Panipat (Afghans beat Marathas). Westminster front: "nabobs"; Lord North's REGULATING ACT 1773 (governor-general, Supreme Court in Calcutta, council majority) → Hastings vs Francis (duel); Hastings impeachment 1786–95. Mir Jafar → Mir Kasim (1760) → war 1763; BAKSAR (Buxar) 24 Oct 1764 (Hector Munro beat Mir Kasim + Shuja-ud-daula) → Bengal independence extinguished. Clive returned May 1765: Shuja restored for 5m Rs; Shah Alam given Allahabad & Kora; imperial farman granting DIWANI of Bengal (1765) — "beginning of British rule"; "dual system". 1770 famine. Hastings governor 1772: stopped dastak fraud, uniform customs reduction, Calcutta capital, revenue via Company servants; saved empire 1780–82 (Hyder Ali invaded Carnatic 1780); acted like ruler of a "country power"; Nandakumar case 1775; Chait Singh, begams of Oudh; Northern Circars; Nawab Muhammad Ali's debts; Treaty of Salbai 1782 (Marathas), Mangalore 1784 (Tipu).
- TEA & COUNTRY TRADE: tea imports London 2.5m lb (1760) → 9m (1769–70) → 6m (American War) → COMMUTATION ACT 1784 (Pitt) → 9m (1784–85) → 14m (1785–86) → 23m lb by 1800. Canton supercargoes sold bills of exchange on London to country traders (less need for Company silver): negligible 1750s → 1m taels (£333,000) 1781–82 → £500,000/yr by 1800. Bengal OPIUM exports in country trade 125,000 Rs (1774–75) → >1m Rs (1786–87). Net China trade profits £234,000 (mid-1770s) → £418,000 (1783–84) → £800–900,000 by 1800. China trade & country trade stabilised British position while "pagoda tree" was shaken. After 1765 belief Indian revenue surplus would buy investment → Company silver to India cut from £400–500,000 to £13–15,000 in 1770s; disillusion early 1780s — military costs; silver back to 1750s level by 1800. Unrequited imports from India ≥£1m/yr; Sinha: £38.4m 1757–80 (~£1.6m/yr). French: no national gain; company liquidated 1769 (30m livres); Isle de France; Suffren's naval successes 1782–83; Bussy. Dutch deficit 21.5m guilders (1761); overseas capital 45m → 28m (1761–79); VOC too long a country trader, blocked Dutch agency houses; debt 67m guilders 1792 → liquidated by state.
- AGENCY HOUSES (after 1763): managed Company servants' savings (9%+ interest), linked land power & sea power; remittances via French, Dutch, Danish companies & "clandestine" Ostend/Trieste/Lisbon voyages; Hastings bought Dutch bills. Cornwallis (1786) "Augean stables" reforms — higher salaries; still dragged into war with Tipu 1789 by country traders & servants; Madras loans at 8–12% from hundreds of Indians, agency magnates, church wardens; Bombay military budget doubled (26 lakh Rs) — Parsis & Poona shroffs. New French company (post-1783) drew £1m of British servants' remittances; Chandernagore factors wanted to live in Calcutta ("headquarters at the English capital?"). 1783–93 100–150 non-company voyages — "British in all but name". 70 British country ships at Cochin 1791–92 (27,000 tons, 4000 lascars, 564 guns); opium to China seas 30 lakh Rs/yr; PENANG 1786 (Francis Light & sultan of Kedah) free emporium. Company servants' gains = "catalyst" of chain reaction; individual enterprise destroying mercantilism & monopoly → free trade. Antonetta voyage 1788–89 (Robert Charnock "burgher of the whole world"); Spanish Philippine Company cartel. Company era closed 1790s: Dutch company liquidated, French opened trade 1790, bankrupt 1795; Pitt's India Act 1784 state control; outports Bristol, Liverpool, Glasgow pressed; charter 1793 concessions. EIC "as a government proceeding from strength to strength but as a company... in eclipse". Seringapatam 4 May 1799 Tipu killed — Wellesley "proconsul of a new empire of conquest"; motto "Tu regere imperio populos, Britanne, memento".
