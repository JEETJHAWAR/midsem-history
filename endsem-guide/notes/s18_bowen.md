# S18 H.V. Bowen, "Bullion for Trade, War, and Debt-Relief: British Movements of Silver to, around, and from Asia, 1760–1833", Modern Asian Studies 44:3 (2010) pp445–475 [OT9]. Bowen: Swansea Univ.
- First detailed assessment of British silver exports to Asia in early phase of imperial expansion. Contrary to some historians, exports at times very considerable esp. after 1785 — used for WAR and DEBT-RELIEF as well as trade; private exports; transfers around Asia; REVERSE flows to Britain after 1810.
- "Bullion for goods" shorthand (Om Prakash's book title, 2004): companies couldn't sell British goods → shipped bullion (S. American silver); up to 80% of cargoes. Richard Campbell, London Tradesman (1747): EIC exports silver & woollens, brings spices, drugs, silks, cottons, diamonds, saltpetre, tea, china; mercantilist critics: "only Superfluities". K.N. Chaudhuri: treasure exports rose to ~£750,000/yr in 1750s; ~65% of exports by 1750s; 70–85% before Seven Years' War (1756).
- Debate: Sushil Chaudhury — bullion impact marginal (appropriated by elites & middlemen); Chaudhuri — active role, injection of income. Gap: no data after 1760. Irfan Habib: British silver to India ceased after 1760 (removing inflationary factor) vs Rajat Datta: continued, rose after 1785 + private — more favourable view of Bengal; Bowen: Datta correct but his figures flawed (Company counted Mocha, Benkulen, Balambangan under "India"). Greenberg & Ralph Davis also erred. Customs ledgers to 1808; records before 1814 destroyed by fire; "East Indies" category lumps destinations. Bowen uses Company Commerce Journals invoice data.
- Company exports: after territorial acquisition, sharp reduction; 1760–1784 just <£2 million, mostly to Canton + small to Benkulen (Fort Marlborough); only 2 consignments to India (Madras 1773, 1776). Directors 1763: more British goods, payments by country powers under treaties, and principally private individuals paying into Indian treasuries for bills of exchange on London (remitting fortunes) → funds for investment. After diwani 1765: specie shipped from India to Canton to buy tea (no direct London→China 1766–68); Bengal "effectually drained of silver" → resumed to 1772; then "COUNTRY SUPPLY" of Canton treasury (Indian goods sold in China + private traders' deposits for bills) → silver from London to China only 1776 between 1771–85; 1780–84 hardly any.
- After mid-1780s: 1785–1820 >£16 million exported; more to INDIA (esp. Bengal after 1800) than China — India £8.5m? (lecture: India £8.50m, China £7.62m of £16.12m). 1784 COMMUTATION ACT cut tea duty to 12½% to beat smuggling → tea demand → silver to China; also sycee (lumps of fine uncoined silver) for Chinese duty payments. Silver to Bombay for raw cotton to Canton (1786, 1803, 1804). WAR: 1793 no silver in Calcutta treasury; 1794 agents couldn't pay advances to weavers & salt producers; Wellesley (1798–1805) expansionism → 1799 £700,000 sent; silver for "augmented investment" diverted "to the purposes of war"; 1802–05 peak, >£1m to Bengal in 1802 and 1804; Cornwallis 1805 emergency ~£500,000; diverted China-bound silver: 1790 £70,000 at Madras for war vs Tipu Sultan; 1805 789,768 oz (£202,281) removed at Madras; 1806 505,298 dollars at Penang → Calcutta. DEBT-RELIEF: 3rd Anglo-Maratha War 1817–18 → 1816–1820 bulk shipments (£875,164 to Bengal 1816–18; £500,000 1819 sinking fund). Irregular "short-term injections". Silver only 18.6% of Company export value 1760–1829; peak 47.9% in 1785–89; 35.1% 1800–04 (commodity export drives under govt pressure). ~half of silver to Canton, rest 3 presidencies. Vansittart lost 1789 (salvagers took 11 of 33 chests). Silver bought from Bank of England / Mocatta & Goldsmid; 1803–04 800,000 dollars at New York; price 5s 1d–5s 8½d/oz.
- PRIVATE flows: 1816 British govt shipped silver to India; commanders, crew, passengers, interlopers; early 18th c capital for Company servants' private trade (Mentz); diamond trade (coral after 1725; >£100,000/yr silver late 1740s; respondentia bonds); £64,000 in 1764–65; Major-General Claude Martin (Lucknow, Awadh) settled indigo accounts in silver: 31,314 oz (1793), 70,000 dollars (1795–96); commanders allowed £3,000 silver for gold at Canton; Archibald Hamilton 1803 37,000 dollars for Charles Forbes & Co and Parsi shipbuilder Pestonji Bomanji. Table 3 private exports 1791–1806 to India £2,674,312. 1802/03–1810/11 India bullion imports: Company 77.1% (£5.55m), private merchants ~£1.42m, commanders 3.1% → ignoring private = underestimating. 1802–11 India silver imports: Britain 43.8%, foreign Europe 16%, America 40.2%; Manila galleon route to China; Kent Deng: China's demand smaller than claimed.
- INTRA-ASIAN: conquest of Bengal 1765 → transfer "tribute" Bengal→Britain via China (tea as remittance). 1767 Bombay 16 chests + Madras 247 chests (24 lakh Rs from Bengal) ~£250,000 to Canton; bills on London to private shippers (1776 £105,129; Cornwallis 1788 12 lakh Rs); 1792–1809 £700,440 to Canton; 1810/11–1827/28 avg Rs 4,31,008/yr India→Canton. REVERSE China→India: almost £2m 1792–1809 (Canton overstocked; high wartime silver price in London); silver forbidden export from China — accumulated surpluses; raw cotton & opium → "golden current" Canton→Malabar; 1810/11–1827/28 avg Rs 68,15,775 (£681,577)/yr Canton→India.
- REVERSE to Britain: 1768 order to send £500,000 (failed — shortage); after 1810 Company's commercial decline (textile imports fell; Indian trade monopoly lost 1813) → home charges from bullion: ~1.5m dollars from China 1811; Bengal £399,732 1811/12; 4.5m dollars 1830–33; Thomas Reid 1817 "temporary expedient" but became regular; 1823 "extraordinary remittance" £2m (silver £602,890 Bengal + gold £1,142,042 Madras/Bombay); Nathan Rothschild bought (£1,114,743 in 1822–23), said bullion best way to transfer tribute; net £8,105,036 1811–33; William Astell 1830. Britain likely net importer of silver from India — "far cry from one-way traffic of bullion for goods".
- Conclusion: tangled web of transfers; silver for war & debt; matters for balance-of-payments & regional economic conditions.
