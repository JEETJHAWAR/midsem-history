# S20 Meera Kosambi & John E. Brush, "Three Colonial Port Cities in India", Geographical Review 78:1 (1988) pp32–47 [OT10]. Kosambi: sociology, Univ. of Poona; Brush: geography, Rutgers.
- Madras, Bombay, Calcutta share distinctive morphology from colonial past: NUCLEUS with European-style FORT + open ESPLANADE, SEGREGATED European & Indian residential areas, CENTRAL BUSINESS DISTRICT, peripheral MILITARY & MANUFACTURAL zones; survived largely intact to early 20th c. Not found in other Indian cities.
- 17th c factories in existing Indian cities (warehouse compounds) had minor, ephemeral effect; but Madras, Bombay, Calcutta = fortified factories at undeveloped village sites fully controlled by EIC → HYBRID towns; Indian & other merchants attracted by commerce under English protection lived segregated. Other colonial urban forms: cantonments, civil lines (Anthony King), hill stations.
- SCHEMATIC MODEL (Fig. 1): fort by commercial waterfront; esplanade; European & Indian residential sectors each with commercial areas; zones for immigrant Asians & Eurasians; peripheral manufactural zone by Indian sector; outlying military zone by European sector. European town always SOUTH of factory & Indian sector (maybe for escape to ships).
- THREE PHASES: (1) fortified factory + white & black towns (Indian town walled at Madras, ditch at Calcutta; Bombay wall enclosed dual town). (2) mid/late 18th c expansion: forts enlarged (ramparts, bastions, ravelins, moats); ESPLANADES for unobstructed gunfire; Indian towns compact near fort; Europeans moved to suburbs 2–5 miles out (horses, carriages, palanquins) — replicating suburbanisation in Britain; fast in Madras & Calcutta, delayed in Bombay. (3) 19th–early 20th c: CBD on earliest English settlement site with European commercial/managerial/admin activity; Indian towns crowded, concentric expansion; INTERMEDIATE zone (Parsis, other Asian immigrants, Eurasians); inner/outer European sectors (outer attracting upper-class Indians); grandiose public buildings (classical & Anglo-Oriental) on esplanades; railway terminals on esplanade land; coal-powered industry (Bombay cotton mills, Calcutta jute mills); congested rental housing, squatters; troops moved to cantonments.
- MADRAS: founded 1639 Coromandel; Fort St George completed 1641 between beach & Elambore river near Cooum; open roadstead (ships anchor a mile offshore); White Town (walled) + Black Town to north (merchants, weavers, dyers). Mid-18th c French occupation → fort strengthened, part of Black Town demolished for esplanade. Late 18th c suburbanisation 3–5 miles arc: Vepery, Egmore, Nungambakkam; Choultry Plain country houses. Fort → administrative; cantonment beyond Adyar (St Thomas Mount, ~8 mi SW). Banking/insurance/wholesale in Black Town (Popham's Broadway); retail ribbon along Mount Road. Indians stayed near Black Town & Triplicane near caste fellows. Black Town renamed GEORGETOWN 1906; Government House; harbour breakwaters 1889/1910. Municipality 27.6 sq mi; Georgetown 1/3 population on 9% area; density 30/acre (1911); sex ratio 102:100; Tamil 63%, Telugu 21%, English 3%; 68% native-born; 1911 Hindu 80, Muslim 12, Christian 8%; Muslims in Triplicane; Christians Vepery, Egmore (Eurasians), Tondiarpet (fisherfolk). Less male-dominated & heterogeneous than Bombay/Calcutta; parklike open land.
- BOMBAY: originally 7 islands merged by filling marshes; Konkan coast; acquired by Charles II from Portugal 1661 (Catherine of Braganza dowry), transferred to EIC 1667; Bombay Castle by deep natural harbour; Bombay Green; semicircular wall with 3 gates → Bombay FORT (name survives); European sector south, Indian north; Gujarati merchants (Hindu, Muslim, Jain, Parsi) encouraged to settle. 1803 FIRE → Native Town outside fort (modelled on Madras/Calcutta); 800-yard esplanade; Colaba suburbs & cantonment (causeway, reclaimed land); later Parel, Malabar Hill (government houses). Rest rural (coconut groves, Mahim, Portuguese Mazagon). Late 19th c: docks on reclaimed land; two railway lines via Salsette; cotton mills north of Native Town (Parel, Byculla). Ramparts removed by 1860s → secretariat, high court, university; Bombay Green → Horniman Circle (CBD node); North Fort Parsi. Native Town density ~700/acre (1911) — far higher than Madras/Calcutta. 1901 pop 776,000: Hindu 65, Muslim 20, Christian 6, Parsi 6%; Marathi 53%, Gujarati 26%, Hindi/Urdu 15%, English 2%. Hierarchy: British + Parsi intermediaries (fort, Colaba, Malabar Hill); Gujarati Hindus & Muslims in Native Town; Marathi Hindus = labour in dockside/industrial tracts. Island site distorted model.
- CALCUTTA: last founded; largest; Hooghly 90 miles of shifting tidal channels; unhealthy. 1686–89 factory at Sutanati (cotton/yarn market) abandoned; Fort William by Red Tank; EIC leased Gobindpur, Calcutta, Sutanati; Maratha Ditch 1742 → later Circular Road + Tolly's Nullah. Plassey 1757; capital of all Company territories 1773; old fort failed in 1757 siege → new Fort William + 2-sq-mile esplanade MAIDAN (Gobindpur lands); Tank Square → DALHOUSIE SQUARE (CBD); Government House 1803; cantonments Barrackpore & Alipore. Suburbs: CHOWRINGHEE, Alipore, Garden Reach; elite shopping/clubs. Indian sector 2/3 of area, 4/5 of population; BURRA BAZAAR — Bengali cloth merchants & moneylenders displaced by north Indian merchants; Hindustani language of commerce; jute labourers; intermediate zone: Anglo-Indians, Portuguese, Armenians, Jews, Parsis, Chinese, Greeks. Jute mills from 1859 at Howrah (railway terminals); Kidderpore wet docks 1890s; 1901 pop 848,000 — largest metropolis. 1911 densities: CBD ~50/acre, Chowringhee <40, Burra Bazaar 280, northern/eastern wards 125–200+. Municipal services first in southern (European) Calcutta. "More in harmony with London than the rest of India". Howrah industry: food, jute, chemicals, shipbuilding, steel.
- CONCLUSION: Madras & Calcutta radial-concentric (Madras westward, Calcutta eastward); Bombay distorted by island; colonial morphology encoded hierarchy & segregation.
