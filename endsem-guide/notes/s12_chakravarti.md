# S12 Chakravarti, "Indic Mercantile Networks and the IO World: A Millennial Overview (c.500–1500)" (Schottenhammer ed. vol I, ch 8, pp191-224); author JNU
- IO ~20% of earth's maritime space; only ENCLOSED ocean; longest sustained seafaring (5 millennia). Monsoon not operative south of 10°S -> IO history = northern sector. Java Sea, Sunda Strait, Gulf of Siam, SCS belong to Pacific but integrated -> "Indo-Pacific rim".
- Few straits: Hormuz, Malacca (Palk unusable) -> choke points replaced by islands, deltas, estuaries.
- Historiography concentrates on 1500–1800/1900 (Portuguese Estado da India + 3 EICs' records, serial data) -> "yawning gap" >1 millennium after 3rd c CE. Pearson: those centuries only tiny part. "Indo-Roman" trade label erroneous (Red Sea, many communities). Bouchon & Lombard hoped for 13th-14th c history.
- Not Indocentric replacing Eurocentric: subcontinent + Sri Lanka at CENTRE of enclosed ocean; voyage between termini never in one season -> two seaboards = stopovers, gateways, transshipment.
- Sources: textual (Indic + foreign), epigraphic, coins, archaeology, visual; two new: Cairo Geniza letters of Jewish "India traders" (Goitein's term; Friedman: India trade "backbone of medieval international economy") and shipwreck archaeology. Qualitative, scattered, rarely quantifiable; combine for "many voices".
- Two messages: (1) Indic peoples DID go to sea, contrary to idea of religious taboo on sea voyage; (2) not only luxuries — daily necessities as bulk.
## 500–800
- Socotra inscriptions (Brahmi, Sanskrit; 1st–5th c; Ingo Strauch) visitors' records: Bhrigukachchha (Barygaza/Broach, Narmada delta), Hastakavapra (Hathab); sailors (navika), navigators (niryamaka); ship drawings, planks stitched with coconut coir, single & double masts; like Satavahana coin ships, Aurangabad Buddhist caves (5th-7th c).
- Cosmas Indicopleustes (Christian Topography, late 6th c): Souppara (Sopara), Kalliena (Kalyan), Sibor (Chaul). Lohatagrama (Kathiawad) in charter of Vishnusena 592 CE granting privileges to merchant body Vaniggrama (= manigramam in S India); vahitra = seagoing ship; Maitrakas of Valabhi.
- Muziris continuity: Byzantine coins at Pattanam.
- Shift: Persian Gulf renewed importance, eclipsing Red Sea (which led 1st c BCE–4th c CE); Sassanian vs Byzantine interests. Chalukya Pulakesin II (610-642) captured Puri (Elephanta) — "goddess of fortune of the western sea"; al-Tabari: diplomacy w/ Khosrow II.
- East: Kaveripattinam (Kaveri delta) peak 5th-6th c (Silappadikaram, Manimekalai). Tamralipta (Tamluk) premier — Faxian sailed from it to Sri Lanka; Yijing: Malay Peninsula–Tamralipta network; Srivijaya; "Ho-lai-ka-lo"=Harikela=Chittagong (proximity to Arakan -> mainland SEA). Dandin Dasakumaracharita (7th c) calls Tamralipta velakula (port). Xuanzang: Samatata (Noakhali-Comilla) contacts w/ 6 SEA places incl Dvaravati. Buddhagupta, master mariner (mahanavika) of Raktamrittika 5th/6th c inscription -> Malay Peninsula. Tamralipta = outlet of landlocked Ganga valley. Dandin's Kalinganagara (Mukhalingam) seashore royal pleasure garden.
## 800–1500
- Arabic/Persian texts: Sulayman (851), Ibn Khurradadhbih, al-Masudi, Buzurg ibn Shahriyar, Hudud al-Alam (982), al-Biruni, al-Marvazi, al-Idrisi, Ibn Battuta. Limitation: most never visited India (except Masudi, Biruni, Battuta) -> stereotypes.
- Abbasid Caliphate (750–870) + Siraf -> Persian Gulf pre-eminent (benefited Konkan, Gujarat). Fatimids in Egypt 969 + Siraf decay early 11th c -> Red Sea renewed; Egypt "hinge" IO–Mediterranean. Hormuz from 13th c revives Gulf.
- Chaudhuri: "emporia trade" + "segmented voyages" after 1000 CE. Belitung wreck early 9th c: stitched western IO ship to China and back with Changsha ceramics -> single-ship whole journey, later replaced by segmented voyages -> advantage to Indian transshipment ports.
- Ports 900–1500: Daibul (Indus delta, Banbhore?), Cambay (Stambhatirtha/Khambat) replaced Bharuch as premier Gujarat port; subsidiary Ghogha and Somanatha (velakula). Somanatha inscriptions 1264 & 1287; al-Biruni: Somanatha links with Zanzibar; 1264: nakhuda Nuruddin Firuz of Hormuz; "Sikottari-mahajanapalli" merchant settlement linked to Socotra.
- Konkan (Aparanta; Arabic Kamkam): indented coast; Sanjan (Samyana), Thana (Sristhanaka; Marco Polo "Tana"), Sopara, Chaul; Aden–Goa network; agrarian growth fuelled ports; Silaharas.
- Malabar (Manibar/Mulaybar): contacts with Aden, Hormuz, Kish. Quilon (Kulam Mali) from 9th c; Calicut surges early 14th c — Ibn Battuta: Chinese vessels + western ships, transshipment.
- GENIZA: Jewish traders from Tunisia -> Fustat (Old Cairo) -> Red Sea ports Qus/Aidhab -> Aden. Aden rep (wakil al-tujjar) Madmun b. Hasan-Japheth (d.c.1151) in shipping business with Sultan Bilal b. Jarir; friend of Abraham Ben Yiju (d.c.1156) — from Maghreb, in Malabar 1132–1149 at Mangalore (al-Manjarur), built a BRONZE factory, married local Tulu woman Ashu (his former slave), 3 children; returned 1149. Slave agent Bama (Tulu; honorific Sheikh) travelled to Egypt. Nakhuda shipowners: Indic ones Fatanswami (Pattanasvami), Ali b. Fawfali (areca-nut dealer turned shipowner), Pdyr/Fidyar (Padiyara). Indic suppliers Sus Siti, Kinbati, Isha, Ishaaq. Itinerant names: Joseph al-Adani al-Mamsawi; Abu Zikri Cohen Sijilmasi; Hiba al-Hamwi. Quote: "One who is present can see what is not seen by one who is absent" (Joseph b. Abraham). Pepper: sale 510.5 dinars; customs tithe 155⅓ dinars at Aden; price 34 vs 23 dinars per bahar (1 bahar=100 lb); shipwreck at Bab-el-Mandeb near Berbera ship of Fatanswami: pepper total loss (c.1138). Seli (Sri Lankan) cinnamon 60 bags/20 bahars. Red cloth lalas, "Kulami lalas" (1135). Iron from Malabar to Aden — only Geniza tells; 5 varieties (bayd ingot "eggs", muhdahth refurbished, rasmi standard, raqs shiny, amlas smooth) + Kufi. Gifts: paper to Ben Yiju (poet/calligrapher). Inseparable bonds: Judah ha-Kohen (1145) told brother-in-law Mahruz, attacked by pirates near Thana, to seek help from Hindu shipowner Tinbu.
- Unniyaticharitam: merchants at Kandiyur, many origins (Kannadar, Kunjarar=Gujarat, Chonakar=Yavana, Chinar, Turikkar) sitting on black carpets with coins; boasting merchant: Arab horse in Chola country = 2000 anayaccu; multilingual.
- Bay of Bengal names: Vangasagara (971 inscription), bahr Harkal (Hudud), Purvasagara. Three segments: Coromandel (Cholas 850–1250), Andhra-Odisha, Bengal; deltas.
- Cholas (peak 985–1150): Nagapattinam replaced Kaveripattinam; Leiden grants (Rajendra 1012–44; Kulottunga 1070–1122). Nagaram trade network at nadu level. Merchant bodies: nanadesi, ubhaya-nanadesi, manigramam, anjuvannam, ainnurruvar (500 svamis of Ayyavole; umbrella org — Karashima & Subbarayalu); charities to shrines. Chola embassies to Song: 1012, 1033, 1077. Kadaram campaign 1025–26: 12 places incl Srivijaya (Palembang), Pannai, Malaiyur, Ilangasoka, Talaittakkolam (Takuapa), Manakkavaram (Nicobar), Kadaram (Kedah) — not annexed; aim to minimise Srivijaya as intermediary w/ Song China ("Zhunian" = Chola realm). Tamil merchant inscriptions overseas 9th–13th c (SL, Myanmar, Thailand, N Sumatra, China); 1088 Barus (camphor) inscription: merchants, shipowners, captains, sailors; no dynastic preamble.
- Vengi/Andhra: Visakhapatnam renamed Kulottungacholapattanam (1068–90) — unique renaming of a port after reigning king; Kulottunga remitted sunka (tolls) — title "sunganadavirttacola". 1090 inscription: Anjuvannam merchant Asavu from Mantai (Sri Lanka); another from Pasai (Sumatra). Kakatiya Ganapati (1199–1261): Motupalli (Krishna delta) charter 1245 abhayasasana — protection from pirates, only fixed duties (kliptasulka); "wealth dearer than life"; Marco Polo praises "Mutfili".
- Bengal: Tamralipta fades after 8th c; Samandar (Chittagong) from mid-9th c — Marco Polo "Bengala", Ibn Battuta "Sudkawan", Ibn Majid "Shatijam"; al-Idrisi: aloes wood, yak tails, rhino horns from Kamarupa (Assam); Ibn Battuta: Sylhet, Sonargaon, boarded junk to China; Sandwip. Fine textiles. Ma Huan & Suma Oriental: cowries from Maldives (currency) exchanged for Bengal rice — bulk cheap goods. Harikela silver coins 7th–12th c non-dynastic, modelled on Chandra coins of Arakan. Rivers: naubandha boat-parking; Devaparvata (Mainamati). Pala Buddhism: Sailendra ruler of Java asks Devapala for land grant to Nalanda; Atisa trained in Suvarnadvipa, sea voyages.
- Transit trade: cinnamon, cardamom, sandal, Sri Lankan ruby; imports gold, silver (Aidhab merchants preferred silver), silk, copper. HORSES: Central Asian "Tatari" overland; from 2nd millennium Arabian & Fars "bahri" (sea-borne) horses; Marco Polo: 10,000/yr to Pandya realm from Shihr, Kish, Hormuz, Aden; 220 gold coins each; landed in Malabar; Tamil horse dealers kudirai-chettis.
- William of Adam (Dominican), How to Defeat the Saracens (c.1316/17) after fall of Acre 1291 -> blockade Aden with galleys at Socotra + Hormuz; body metaphor: Indian sea = head, Aden = throat, Red Sea/Egypt = stomach; India "source of all evils" (wealth for Mamluk Egypt). Aden trade with Collam, Tana, Cambayet, "dive insulide" (Diu or Maldives). Never implemented.
- Mediterranean = mare nostrum, navies; IO rulers saw land as arena; IO "arena for merchants, sailors, pirates, fisher-folk", not rulers' naval contest before 1500 (exceptions prove rule) -> mare liberum not mare clausum.
- Plurality: Rashtrakuta Konkan: Sirafi, Baghdadi, Omani Muslims built mosques, qadis at Saimur & Sindan; Chola Saiva kings granted land for Buddhist Chudamani vihara at Nagapattinam at Srivijayan request; Kerala grants to church & synagogue, witnesses signed Pahlavi, Hebrew, Arabic. Somanatha 1264 bilingual Sanskrit–Arabic inscription: mosque (mijigiti) by Nuruddin Firuz with Hindu "dharma-bandhava" (righteous friends), endorsed by Saiva priest & Chaulukya admin; Allah called Visvarupa, Visvanatha, Sunyarupa, Lakshyalakshya.
