# S12 al-Sharida, "Merchants without Borders: Qusman Traders in the Arabian Gulf and the IO, c.1850–1950", Arabian Humanities 17 (2023) [OT8]
- Context: early 19th c Britain master power; 1820 peace agreement (General Treaty) with Gulf Arab rulers -> British control India–Arabia trade + "commercial security"; Gulf protectorates ruled from India (Viceroy); steamships 2nd half 19th c + Suez 1869 -> Gulf re-integrated in global economy (pearls, dates to Europe/N America). Arabia–India trade "fully recovered" after cut by Portuguese early 16th c.
- Najdis migrated to Gulf ports (Kuwait, Bahrain) then India (Karachi, Bombay, Calcutta). Those linked to Gulf ports = ahl al-hadra / al-hadrat ("people of downward"). Qusman (sing. Qasimi) = people of al-Qasim, one of 3 districts of Najd; towns Buraydah & Unayzah.
- Network: Cairo, Damascus, Gaza, Jerusalem, Baghdad, Basra, Kuwait, Bahrain, al-Uqayr, al-Jubayl, Mecca, Madinah, Jidda, Karachi, Bombay, Calcutta (+ Oman, E Africa).
- Minority vs Kuwaitis/Bahrainis etc, but majority vs rest of Najd/Hijaz. Two trades of al-Qasim: al-Uqaylat (overland camel export to Iraq, Sham, Egypt; 18th c start; studied) and al-hadrat (Gulf/India; under-studied — his contribution).
- Peter Valenti: al-Qasim an ENTREPOT in Najd -> Najd incorporated into global economy by 19th c "if not earlier". Unusual: desert people in maritime trade, became influential.
- Najd poor -> imports: clothes, rice, coffee, tea, sugar, cardamom, kerosene; caravans 3–4 round trips/yr, 20 days one way; late 1920s 15–20 Maria Theresa Dollars per camel; camel ~440 lb = 2 bags sugar. Chain: India Qusman -> Bahrain Qusman -> al-Jubayl/al-Uqayr -> camel caravan -> al-Qasim (e.g. al-Suhaymi of Unayzah at al-Jubayl receives from Abd al-Aziz b. Ali al-Bassam of Bahrain).
- Commodities: "anything available and profitable": slaves, horses, pearls, gold, currency, weapons, textiles, carpets, dates, spice, rice, sugar, tea, coffee, perfume.
- Slaves: Najd small market (Al Rashid of Ha'il used slaves as soldiers, Al Saud as guards); Abd Allah al-Khunayni (Doughty met 1877) brought slaves from Zanzibar; Sulayman al-Rawwaf Baluchi slaves via Oman.
- Horses: Bombay greatest foreign market for Arab horses; Burckhardt 1814–15; Khurshid Pasha letter 1839: British buying horses from Qasim since 1825; Unayzah collecting centre (marbat) from Bedouin tribes (Philby 1918); Lorimer: Najd principal horse-breeding country, Qasim the export outlet; bought in winter before monsoon, driven to Kuwait; ~Rs 300 in Gulf, Rs 200 transport, Rs 600–900 in Bombay (Byculla market — Times of India 1894 "no market in the world... like our own Byculla"); Bengal Rs 1800–2500. al-Shubayli family; Al Ibrahim of Kuwait raced in Bombay.
- Pearls: demand in Europe/N America late 19th c; Qusman mostly as poor divers (not maritime people); Muqbil al-Dhukayr of Unayzah in Bahrain entered ~1870, leading by 1890, sold in India; "Fakhr al-Tujjar" bankrupt 1917, died c.1922. Collapse: WWI 1914, cultured pearls 1925, Depression 1929. Bahrain pearl exports 1914 Rs 15 m. Abdallah al-Fawzan (Bombay, 1861–1960) letters on depressed markets.
- Gold & currency: after pearl collapse; Swiss gold Rs 50 in Gulf -> Rs 100–120 India; sterling Rs 28 India -> Rs 60–107 Gulf; profits 300–400%; British banned -> smuggling hidden in boom boats via nakhuda; Hamad al-Bassam gold smuggler Bahrain->India; Salih al-Bassam currency; 1928 letter: 1,000 Ottoman majidis sold in Bombay for Rs 997 via Bahrain (Ottoman collapse + Saudi conquest of Hijaz).
- Weapons: British ban; al-Sulaym carried rifles Muscat->Kuwait (exile 1891 battle of al-Mulayda –1902); Sulayman al-Rawwaf (b.1908) smuggled from Dar es Salaam/Zanzibar, spoke Hindi, jailed by British, escaped.
- Textiles: Doughty 1877 — 200-camel caravan from Basra; Lorimer: Buraydah "entirely" dependent on Kuwait for cotton goods. Salih b. Abdallah al-Bassam arrived Bombay 1887 aged 25; from 1928 agent for red shimagh (kufiyya) -> brand "Shimagh al-Bassam" (nephew Muhammad b. Ali al-Bassam c.1897–1985; house in Pune). Al-Assafi house in Baghdad/Basra: Indian textiles (187 rolls Surat textile from Indian merchant Hasan Bahay 1898, redistributed to Jewish Baghdadi traders), Manchester 400 rolls 1884; agent for Ibn Rashid of Ha'il (textiles as gifts 1902 after Ibn Saud took Riyadh). Japanese textiles via Bahrain. Carpets: al-Zamil of Bahrain 1929 order of 40 zuliyya + 40 bisat to Madinah via al-Fadil in Jeddah — always dealing with fellow Unayzah men.
- Dates: al-suluq (cooked dried Khunayzi dates of Qatif) to India; al-Assafi 400 sacks brand "SS" to Beirut, Jaffa, Mersin, Haifa (1901).
- Perfume (6 jars from Calcutta 1899), spices (al-Assafi 54 + 205 sacks black pepper 1881), rice (al-Khunayni; al-Fawzan shipped 1,020 sacks Calcutta->Jeddah 1884; 50/50 profit sharing al-Dhukayr/al-Assafi; profits kept as credit with agents), sugar (al-Khunayni "navigated for sugar to Mauritius", fortune £24,000; al-Mushayqih 10,000 sacks; rain problem — jammal said "the rains took it"), tea (Calcutta al-Bassam brothers -> Bahrain 1944), coffee (150 sacks Javanese coffee from Malabar/Bombay 1884; rain-spoiled 80-camel load given by al-Mushayqih as Ibn Saud's gifts to Bedouins), glass coffee cups bought for few halala sold 25 halala.
- Methods: family firms, agents (sons sent to India yearly), correspondence/letters as trust, commercial marks (SS, HMB), credit kept with agents, deal with fellow Qasimis/relatives but also Indians & Jews.
- Sources: al-Assafi commercial records (Imam Muhammad b. Saud Univ., Riyadh), family letters (al-Umari, Wathaiq... 300 letters), personal interviews 2018, travellers Doughty, Philby, Palgrave, Lorimer's Gazetteer, Altorki & Cole interviews.
- Conclusion: poverty pushed migration; generations; networks Egypt→India; founded leading companies.
