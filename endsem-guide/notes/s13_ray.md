# S13 H.P. Ray, "Warp and Weft: Producing, Trading and Consuming Indian Textiles Across the Seas (1st–13th c CE)", ch 11 in Machado, Fee & Campbell eds, Textile Trades, Consumer Cultures... (2018) pp289-311 [R10]. Ray: LMU Munich.
- IO commodity categories: aromatics/medicines/dyes/spices; foodstuffs, wood, textiles; gems/ornaments; metals; plant/animal products. Sources Periplus (1st c) to Geniza (11–12th c), Chinese, Arab. Goitein surveyed 311 documents; 77 items Arabia/E Africa/India→Cairo; staples: textiles, beads, ivory, metal-ware.
- Cotton deep history: cultivated cotton (Gossypium arboreum tree cotton, G. herbaceum) at Mehrgarh, 5th millennium BCE; Mohenjo-daro silver vases wrapped in red-dyed cotton (Marshall); Dorian Fuller: spinning increase in peninsula 2nd half 2nd millennium BCE; jute cloth Harappa 2200–1900 BCE.
- Three aspects: (1) diverse varieties, only SOME travelled; (2) organisation of trade networks, changes 1st–14th c; (3) multiple uses of cloth within subcontinent. Big issue: textiles as means of COMMUNICATION across shared cultural ethos; markers of identity; create social relationships; ritual significance (Java 9th–10th c inscriptions).
- New archaeology: Tha Kae near Lopburi, Thailand: 90 spindle whorls 3rd c BCE–3rd c CE (Judith Cameron) -> India link; Pontanoa, Bangka (Sulawesi): 24 fragments c.500 CE, block-printed & resist dyed — unique Indian process.
- Consumption in India: Later Vedic: cotton daily, woollen shawls kambala, fine linen ksauma for elite. Arthasastra (50–125 CE) treasury textiles: woollens, Nepal, animal hair, dukula, linen, cotton, silks — patrorna, kauseya (wild silk), cina (Chinese Bombyx silk). Panini mentions kauseya.
- Buddhism: cloth among 4 permissible gifts (robes, food, rest place, medicine); only cotton at first (distinct identity); King Pasenadi gifted fine linen shawl to a nun -> controversy; later silk allowed = "middle way"; silk in relic casket Devnimori (Gujarat); Kanheri cave 11 inscription 12 Sept 854: devotee from Gauda (Bengal) endowed 100 drammas for meditation room & monks' clothing at Krishnagiri mahavihara.
- Tamil Sangam: 2381 poems by 473 poets (1st c BCE–3rd c CE); akam/puram; Purananuru (400 poems): white cloth covers dead hero; embroidered silk gifts by king to bard; woman spinning cotton by small fire.
- Periplus: "cloth of all kinds", garments, silks from west coast to Socotra, S Arabia. Geniza (459 docs, 1080–1160): Indian red silk/muslin lanas/lalas mostly as PRESENTS (wives, friends, religious dignitaries); cotton traded in quantity but "secondary"; only precious textiles worth cargo space; Jewish traders catered to middle class. Futa (sarong) sent Egypt→Aden→India for foreigners resident in India (linen/silk) while Indians wore cotton.
- INDIGO oldest organic dye; Periplus export from Barbarikon; Greek indikon melan "Indian black"; Sanskrit nila -> SE Asia; Arabic an-nila. Dharmasutras (3rd–2nd c BCE): snataka should avoid dyed clothes (Apastamba); Vasistha: brahman white, ksatriya madder red, vaisya yellow silk; Manusmriti X.89: brahman in distress may trade but avoid dyed cloth, indigo (nili), lac. Buddhist monks reddish-brown; no indigo, turmeric etc. Susruta: indigofera + ghee antidote; Panchatantra "Blue Jackal" Candarava falls in indigo vat. Kavya (Sri Harsa's Naisadhiyacarita 11th c) ignores clothing. vs. inscriptions/sculpture/painting: Mathura sataka cloth & dyer's wife donor; Bharhut, Sanchi ornamental cloth; Ajanta (5th c) patterned tunics (hamsa, rosettes, chevrons, silver brocade).
- Technique: mordant-painting + resist dyeing; mordant (fixative) because cotton doesn't take dye; applied with qalams (pens) or wooden blocks; resist = molten wax or moist mud ("negative"). Nyaya-Vaisesika philosophers vs Buddhists (by 10th c) debated colour of multicoloured cloth -> "citra" (variegated).
- Divyavadana: shops at Sopara for Kasi (Varanasi) cloth, phuttaka (printed muslin); puspapatta flowered cloth; Siyadoni inscription (903–968) textile market shops; Piranmalai (13th c, Sokkanatha temple) cess on cotton, yarn, thick/thin cloth; Mandasor inscription 5th c: silk weavers' guild migrated, kept identity; "varied stripes of different colours".
- BERENIKE (Red Sea, Egypt): founded 1st half 3rd c BCE, abandoned before c.550 CE (~800 yrs); ~800 textile pieces (360 in 1995, 450 in 1996); 48% cotton; Roman Egypt S-spin vs half of Berenike cotton Z-spun = Indian; blue-resist dyed cotton floral fragment (1994); small Z-spun cotton bag; only one spindle whorl -> CONSUMER city; indigo plants present; multicultural (Egypt, Mediterranean, Axum, S Arabia, Nabataea, Palmyra, India?), Greek lingua franca; Nikanor Archive (camel-owner family); commerce "free-wheeling", barter; no clear Roman official involvement.
- Fustat (Abbasid capital 750–969): ~3000 fragments 750–1100; blue-white elephant & tree panel. Quseir al-Qadim (1982 excavations): Arabic letters on paper 1200–1240; Indian blue textiles "tree of life" + elephant / rosette prized at home. Newberry collection (Ashmolean, Oxford; collected Cairo 1920–32): 1226 printed pieces; 267 blue, 253 blue+red; radiocarbon 10th–16th c; designs like architectural ornament & Jain manuscript covers -> GUJARAT origin; used as garments, furnishings, room-dividers, door hangings, canopies.
- Organisation: assumption that kinship-based trade changed only after 13th c (big states, taxes, markets) — Ray questions. Periplus: no state control over maritime trade. Arthasastra: Superintendent of Yarns employs women spinners. Nasik inscription yr 42: Usavadata (son-in-law of Kshaharata satrap Nahapana) endowed 3000 kahapanas for Sangha — invested in weavers' guild at Govardhana (2000 at 1 pratika % monthly = "cloth money"), interest only used; registered at town hall. Sulka = toll (sulkika collector) for roads, ferries, harbours. Vishnusena charter 592 from Lohata (Kathiawar, Maitrakas 493–776): protects vaniggrama; 72 trade regulations; lower tax for religious goods; tax on pressing indigo. Ibn Awkal business house (980–1030) shipped indigo India→Egypt.
- 9th–mid-14th c guilds: Ainurruvar, Manigramam, Nanadesi, Anjuvannam + craftsmen (weavers etc.); inscriptions cluster Dharwad–Bijapur, Mysore, Thanjavur, Tiruchirapalli, Madurai; private armies; donations to temples, tanks. Kochi plates of Bhaskara Ravivarman (early 11th c): title Anjuvannam + privileges to Jew Joseph Rabban at Muyirikkodu (Pattanam); Kottayam plates of Vira-Raghava (13th c): title Manigramam to Ravikkoran of Kodangalur. Anjuvannam = Hanjamana (Konkan) = West Asian sea-going merchants, to Java; from 9th c; by 11th c mostly Muslim; ends 13th c (Subbarayalu). Assemblies: pattana-dharmayam/pagudi (shared contribution to temples) and erivira-pattinam (towns of merchants + soldiers, 11th–12th c TN -> guards more important).
- Tamil inscriptions SE Asia (8 known, mid-9th–late-13th c): near Pagan, 2 south of Isthmus of Kra, 4 north & west Sumatra, 1 China. Earliest Takua Pa (9th c): tank + military camp + Saiva figures. Lobo Tuwa near Barus 1088: tax on captains & crew. 12th c north Sumatra: regulations on losses, interest waived. Quanzhou bilingual Tamil–Chinese at Siva temple (late 13th c).
- Java/Bali banigrama (10th c; 7 Javanese inscriptions 902–1053; Julah in Bali) indigenous, tax-farmers. Javanese statuary 10th–11th c shows Indian export cloth patterns; sima (land status change) ceremonies: 200 pieces of cloth gifted; ~70 varieties; red or blue most common; place of origin noted; status & sex considered.
- Multiple meanings: cotton cloth (karpasika-pata) as writing material — Nearchos (326 BCE) Indians wrote on beaten cotton; prepared with wheat/rice paste, polished with conch; Rajasthan almanacs; Kerala traders' accounts; Karnataka kaditam (tamarind paste + charcoal, chalk pencils). Documents (lekhyam) legal; tax kovera. Ben Yiju wrote on CLOTH in India when no paper (2 pieces in Geniza).
- Conclusion: textiles not mere luxury to pay for spices nor just made "to requirements of foreign consumer" (John Guy, Woven Cargoes 1998) — integral to the maritime network since start of CE; value varied in time & place; no generalisations: SEA textiles stored wealth vs India gold/silver jewellery; Java inscriptions mention cloth because of land-donation rituals; study micro (local) + macro (IO) contexts.
